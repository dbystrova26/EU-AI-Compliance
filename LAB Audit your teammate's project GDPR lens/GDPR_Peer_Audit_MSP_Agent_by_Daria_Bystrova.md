## Phase 1: Read and Annotate

Key observations from the data processing brief:

- **Personal data categories present:** Content of topics submitted by the practitioner (text input, may reference client types or scenarios); AI-generated content drafts stored in Airtable under the practitioner's account; practitioner's Gmail address (used for n8n notifications); potentially the practitioner's brand identity and voice embedded in the system prompt or source documents.
- **Special-category risk:** The MSP source documents cover psychological and transformational concepts. If source documents reference named individuals, case studies, or client scenarios — even pseudonymised — special-category health or psychological data could be embedded in the Pinecone vector store and reproduced in generated content. The brief does not confirm or rule this out.
- **EU border crossings:** OpenAI (US), Pinecone (US), Airtable (US), n8n Cloud (EU-hosted but check), Render/Railway (US), Tavily (US). All AI inference and storage infrastructure is US-based.
- **Purpose compatibility flag:** The MSP source documents were created as educational/methodological texts. They are now being used to train a commercial content generation pipeline for a specific practitioner. This is a different purpose from the original creation of those documents — copyright and purpose limitation both need examination.
- **Potential incompatible reuse:** If the practitioner's past client communication, session notes, or case material has been uploaded as source documents, processing that data to generate public-facing marketing content would be a highly incompatible secondary purpose.

## Phase 3: Clarifying Questions Log

**Question 1 — Do any of the MSP source documents contain personal data about identifiable individuals — for example, case studies, client vignettes, session transcripts, or named research participants?**

*Why it matters:* If yes, this is the most significant GDPR risk in the entire system. Source document content is chunked, embedded in Pinecone, and retrieved to generate public-facing content. If those chunks contain personal data about real people (even pseudonymised clients), that data could be reproduced in Instagram captions or LinkedIn posts — a highly incompatible secondary purpose, and potentially a special-category data issue under Article 9 for health or psychological data. The lawful basis for the original processing (therapeutic relationship or research consent) almost certainly does not extend to marketing content generation.

*Provisional assumption:* The source documents are methodological texts (books, frameworks, conceptual descriptions) without personal data about identifiable individuals. If this assumption is wrong, the compliance picture changes significantly and a stop recommendation would apply.

**Question 2 — Where is n8n Cloud hosted — EU or US servers?**

*Why it matters:* n8n offers both EU and US hosting options. If EU-hosted, the n8n processing does not create an EU-to-US transfer issue. If US-hosted, it does, and a DPA with transfer mechanism is required. The brief does not specify.

*Provisional assumption:* n8n Cloud is EU-hosted (n8n's default for European customers is Frankfurt). If this is wrong, the transfer map expands.

**Question 3 — Has the practitioner obtained any consent or permission from the owners of the MSP source documents for commercial use in a content generation pipeline?**

*Why it matters:* The source documents are described as "books by authors working on MSP" — implying third-party authored works. Using copyrighted content in a commercial RAG pipeline raises copyright questions (not GDPR, but parallel legal risk). More relevantly for GDPR: if any of those authors are identifiable natural persons and their content is being commercially exploited, a purpose limitation question arises about the original purpose of their published work versus its use here.

*Provisional assumption:* The practitioner has permission or the documents are the practitioner's own work. Flagged for legal review.

**Question 4 — Are the two MSP websites (mentalspaceresearch.com, somsp.com) aware that their content is being automatically retrieved and incorporated into commercial social media content?**

*Why it matters:* Automated retrieval and commercial incorporation of website content may require the site owners' consent depending on their terms of use and applicable copyright law. If either site contains personal data (named practitioners, researchers, or clients), the retrieval also creates a GDPR question about the purpose of that processing.

*Provisional assumption:* The sites are operated by parties within the MSP community who have implicitly or explicitly consented to this use. Not confirmed.

**Question 5 — Is there any mechanism for the practitioner to label AI-generated content before publishing it to their audience?**

*Why it matters:* Article 50(1) of the EU AI Act (not GDPR, but relevant to the law stacking check) requires disclosure that content is AI-generated unless obvious from context. The brief describes a human review step in Airtable but no disclosure mechanism for the final audience. This is the primary AI Act gap and indirectly relevant to the GDPR transparency principle.

*Provisional assumption:* No disclosure mechanism exists. Flagged as AI Act gap; noted here for completeness.

## Phase 4: Audit Report

### Section 1: System Summary

The Mental Space Psychology AI Storytelling Agent is an automated content drafting tool built for an MSP practitioner. The system takes a topic input, retrieves relevant content from a private Pinecone vector database of uploaded MSP source documents and from two pre-approved websites, and uses OpenAI to generate a multi-platform content package including Instagram captions, LinkedIn posts, and carousel outlines. Drafts are stored in Airtable and the practitioner receives an email notification to review them. The system runs on a two-day automated schedule. No content is published automatically — the practitioner must change the Airtable status to "Approved" before any publication. The practitioner is the controller. All AI inference, storage, and automation infrastructure is US-based.

---

### Section 2: Data and Role Map

The system processes the following personal data: topic input text (low risk unless referencing named individuals); MSP source document content embedded in Pinecone (potentially higher risk if documents contain personal data about clients or research participants); AI-generated content drafts stored in Airtable; and the practitioner's email address used for notifications.

The practitioner (Julia G) is the controller. Six processors receive personal data: OpenAI, Pinecone, Airtable, n8n Cloud, Render/Railway, and Tavily. All are US-based (subject to n8n hosting clarification). No DPAs are documented or confirmed in place with any of them. All EU-to-US data flows require a transfer mechanism — SCCs or DPF certification — which is not documented.

### Section 3: Compliance Findings

---

**Finding 1 — DPAs absent for all processors**
**Severity: Blocking**

**Description:** GDPR Article 28 makes a written DPA with every processor a legal requirement — not a best practice. The system sends personal data (topic inputs containing potentially identifying content, source document chunks, AI-generated drafts) to six processors: OpenAI, Pinecone, Airtable, n8n Cloud, Render/Railway, and Tavily. No DPAs are documented or confirmed in place with any of them. Processing cannot lawfully begin without these agreements.

**Recommended action:** Establish DPAs with all six processors before any personal data flows. Most major vendors (OpenAI, Airtable, Google) provide standard DPAs — this is primarily an administrative task. Smaller vendors (Tavily, Railway) should be checked for DPA availability; if unavailable, the vendor relationship should be reconsidered.

**Escalation needed?** No for standard vendor DPAs. Yes — legal review recommended — if any vendor cannot provide a compliant DPA.

---

**Finding 2 — International transfer mechanisms undocumented**
**Severity: Blocking**

**Description:** All five non-Google processors are US-based. EU-to-US data transfers require a valid transfer mechanism: an adequacy decision (does not apply to the US generally), Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs), or DPF certification by the recipient. The brief provides no evidence that any transfer mechanism is in place or has been assessed. Without a valid mechanism, EU personal data cannot lawfully be transferred to these vendors.

**Recommended action:** For each US processor, confirm whether they are DPF-certified (check the DPF list at dataprivacyframework.gov). If certified, document this. If not, execute the 2021 SCCs as part of the DPA. Complete this in parallel with Finding 1 — the DPA and transfer mechanism are typically documented together.

**Escalation needed?** No for standard SCCs. Yes — if the EU-US DPF is challenged or invalidated in future, alternative mechanisms must be prepared rapidly.

---

**Finding 3 — Source document content in Pinecone: lawful basis and special-category risk unresolved**
**Severity: Significant (Blocking if assumption in Q1 is wrong)**

**Description:** The system embeds MSP source documents into Pinecone for RAG retrieval. If any of these documents contain personal data about identifiable individuals — case studies, client vignettes, session transcripts — then: (a) a lawful basis is required for storing and processing that personal data; (b) if the data includes psychological or health information, an Article 9 condition is required; (c) using that data to generate public-facing marketing content would be a highly incompatible secondary purpose, almost certainly not covered by any original processing purpose. The brief describes the documents as "books by MSP authors" but does not confirm they contain no personal data.

**Recommended action:** Before embedding any document in Pinecone, review it for personal data — particularly named individuals, client references, or case study content. Any document containing personal data about identifiable individuals should be excluded from the RAG pipeline unless a specific lawful basis and Article 9 condition (if applicable) can be documented for the new purpose.

**Escalation needed?** Yes — legal review required if any source documents contain health, psychological, or case study data about identifiable individuals.

---

**Finding 4 — No DPIA conducted**
**Severity: Significant**

**Description:** The system triggers multiple EDPB DPIA criteria: it uses innovative AI technology (LLM + RAG + automated scheduling) for commercial content generation; it combines multiple data sources; it involves cross-border transfers to US processors; and it processes content in a psychologically sensitive domain. At least three EDPB criteria are met. A DPIA is required before production deployment.

**Recommended action:** Conduct a DPIA covering: description of processing, necessity and proportionality assessment, risks to data subjects (including the source document personal data risk), proposed mitigations (DPAs, transfer mechanisms, document review), and residual risks. The DPIA should be completed before the system processes any EU personal data.

**Escalation needed?** No for the DPIA itself — can be conducted internally with a structured template. Yes if the DPIA reveals high residual risks requiring supervisory authority consultation under Article 36.

---

**Finding 5 — No privacy notice for data subjects**
**Severity: Significant**

**Description:** The system processes personal data about the practitioner (email address, potentially brand and voice data). If source documents contain data about third parties (clients, research participants), those individuals have no notice of processing. GDPR Articles 13 and 14 require that data subjects be informed of processing. The brief describes no privacy notice.

**Recommended action:** Provide a privacy notice covering: what data is processed, for what purpose, on what lawful basis, with which processors, and for how long. For third parties whose data may be in source documents, indirect notice obligations under Article 14 apply.

**Escalation needed?** No.

---

**Finding 6 — Retention periods undefined**
**Severity: Minor**

**Description:** No retention periods are stated for Airtable draft records, Pinecone embeddings, or email notifications. GDPR Article 5(1)(e) requires that personal data not be kept longer than necessary.

**Recommended action:** Define retention periods: recommend 12 months for Airtable drafts (then deletion), indefinite for Pinecone embeddings only if source documents contain no personal data, and standard email retention for notifications.

**Escalation needed?** No.

### Section 5: Overall Recommendation

**Do not proceed — until two blocking findings are resolved.**

The system has two blocking findings that must be resolved before any EU personal data is processed: DPAs are absent for all six processors (Finding 1), and international transfer mechanisms are undocumented for all US-based vendors (Finding 2). These are not design gaps — they are legal prerequisites for lawful processing. The system may also have a third blocking issue depending on the content of the MSP source documents embedded in Pinecone (Finding 3). Once Findings 1 and 2 are resolved and Finding 3 is assessed, the system can proceed with the remaining significant findings addressed concurrently or shortly after go-live.

**Conditions to move to "proceed with conditions":**
1. DPAs in place with all six processors
2. Transfer mechanisms documented (DPF certification or SCCs) for all US processors
3. Source documents reviewed and confirmed to contain no personal data about identifiable individuals (or, if they do, a compliant lawful basis documented)

## Stretch: Remediation Plan — Finding 1 (DPAs Absent for All Processors)

**What specific artifact closes this gap?**
A signed Data Processing Agreement with each of the six processors: OpenAI, Pinecone, Airtable, n8n Cloud, Railway/Render, and Tavily. Each DPA must include: the subject matter and duration of processing; the nature and purpose of processing; the type of personal data and categories of data subjects; the controller's obligations and rights; sub-processor restrictions; and (for US vendors) Standard Contractual Clauses as the transfer mechanism or confirmation of DPF certification.

**Who would own it?**
The controller — Julia G (the MSP practitioner). As the deployer and controller, she bears the legal obligation to establish DPAs with processors acting on her behalf. The builder (Julian) can assist by identifying which vendors need DPAs and locating their standard DPA documents, but the controller must sign.

**Realistic timeline:**
3–5 business days for the standard vendor DPAs (OpenAI, Airtable, Google all provide self-service DPA acceptance). Tavily and Railway should be checked within the same period — if they cannot provide compliant DPAs, the vendor choice should be reconsidered. Total: one week for a competent team.

**Evidence to confirm gap is closed to a regulator:**
- Signed DPA documents (or DPA acceptance confirmation emails) for each processor, dated before first processing run
- For US vendors: either DPF certification listing printout or signed SCCs appended to the DPA
- A record of processing activities (Article 30 register) listing all processors, their roles, and the transfer mechanism for each
- Confirmation that the DPAs have been reviewed by legal counsel or that the practitioner has taken independent legal advice on the terms
